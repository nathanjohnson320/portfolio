# What and Why

> We are offloading too much cognitive effort onto LLMs and this is making the software industry as a whole worse.

I am speaking mainly from the perspective of a corporate web developer. Not an academic or someone who can write code the old ways. But what I'm seeing is this maximal offloading of our thoughts onto agents to do work. There is no attempt to understand what the agent is doing or why, no attempt to guide the agent in a meaningful way, there is only a maximum focus on output of features for our corporate overlords.

The effects of this are only beginning to be felt.

* Features that were made 2 weeks ago where no one remembers the what or the why.
* Incidents where people sit on the call waiting for claude to propose solutions instead of diving into the terminal with existing knowledge to patch.
* Giving up on reviews to let agents maintain the codebase resulting in complete dependence on frontier agents to do any task

There are more of these, but these are the main ones I notice. We're so dependant on agents to perform our jobs that we are collectively forgetting how to build the systems. And when we forget how to build the systems we then have to relearn the skills to build. But someone ultimately at the end of the day **HAS** to understand the what and why of the system itself. If nobody understands the what and why, then eventually there will be a reckoning of the product itself. LLMs are not maintainable long term and the code bases they create are not maintainable long term. They have contextual limits that fundamentally prevent this. Anyone saying otherwise has no idea what they're talking about and are either new to the industry and blindly following or they are boomed out and gave up.
